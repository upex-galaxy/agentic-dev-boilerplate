00 · the blueprint before the build

From blueprint to product, with AI in between.

/design-system works in two moments. First, once per project, it fixes the system: the tokens in DESIGN.md (tokens mode). Then, story by story and right before building it, it commissions the mockup of that screen from a design tool, you ratify it, and the AI maps it into the plan that guides /sprint-development (mode screen). If the app already exists and has its own look, the extract mode writes that DESIGN.md from the live theme instead of choosing it.

for whoever shapes the story, not only whoever codes it modes: tokens · extract · screen tools: Claude Design · Open Design · Figma · manual HTML · design skill (Mode C) engine: Claude Code · OpenCode · Codex · Pi + skills

01 · mental model

Two blueprints that never get mixed up

The most common mistake is mixing the design system with the screen design. They are two artifacts, with two owners and two different consumers, and in fact two different physical "drop zones" in the repo.

System · defined once

DESIGN.md

./DESIGN.md

The tokens: palette, typography, spacing, radii, components. The "visual voice" of the whole product.

  • /design-system generates it in tokens mode, through one of five paths, A to E (manual gallery, getdesign + matcher, Open Design, Claude Design or custom). Details in §02. In an app that already exists, the extract mode writes it from its live theme (§02·c).
  • Open format (Google Labs), any agent can read it.
  • /project-bootstrap consumes it when it sets up the frontend.
  • Its bundle drop zone is design/handoff/<slug>/ (repo root): do not confuse it with the screens one.
Screens · done per story

master-design-plan.md

.context/design/master-design-plan.md

The spec per screen (§4) + the Story → Screen map (§8). It says which screen each story renders and how it should look.

  • /design-system builds it in screen mode (opt-in, it always asks) from mockups that you ratify.
  • The AI never writes a mockup by hand without a design skill loaded: it commissions it from a design tool, writes it through that skill (Mode C) or you bring it.
  • /sprint-development consumes it in every UI story (repo Rule 14).
  • Its drop zone for raw mockups is .context/designs/<project>/<batch>/, plural "designs", different from the singular "design" of the plan.
Golden rule: DESIGN.md rules over tokens; the master design plan rules over screens. Values are never duplicated: the screens plan points to DESIGN.md as the authority for color and typography. And watch the folder names: design/handoff/ (tokens bundle) vs. .context/designs/ (screen mockups) vs. .context/design/ (the finished plan): three folders, three different payloads.
02 · tokens phase

The system, once per project

tokens mode. It runs once, after the PRD and before the SRS (/project-foundation calls it in its phase 2.5), and again only if the business pivots or wants to rebrand without re-scaffolding. It leaves DESIGN.md at the root: the frozen contract that everything else respects.

Path When to pick it What the AI does · what you do
B · getdesign + matcher default There is a PRD and you want something from a catalog, good quality, no manual effort. The AI ranks three candidates from the catalog that npx getdesign list returns; you pick one or ask for another batch.
A · Gallery manual You want to explore and choose without the AI deciding, or the matcher does not convince you. You browse designmd.ai/explore or npx getdesign list; the AI downloads and lints what you picked.
C · Open Design app You want to iterate visually in a local UI before fixing tokens. You iterate in the desktop app (Docker is an alternative, not a requirement); the AI waits and then converts the result via E.
D · Claude Design You have a Pro plan or higher and want the best visual quality. You design in claude.ai/design and export the bundle to design/handoff/; the AI converts it via E.
E · LLM-authored The business is very specific and nothing in the catalog fits. The AI writes DESIGN.md from scratch following the spec, with the Constitution and the PRD as input.
02·b · the default path, step by step

Reads the business context

business-model.md, personas.md and executive-summary.md. If they do not exist and the app has no theme in the code either, it asks you five questions: industry, tone, target, competitors and keywords. With a theme in the code, the path is the extract mode.

Ranks the catalog

It fetches the catalog with npx getdesign list --json and runs scripts/match-brand.ts: three candidates with a score and a reason.

You choose

Name, tagline and preview hint for each one. You pick one, or ask for "more options" and the next batch of three arrives.

you → "the second one"

Download and lint

npx getdesign add <slug> leaves DESIGN.md at the root and npx @google/design.md lint DESIGN.md validates it. If it fails, it shows you the error and offers to fix it by hand or retry.

The format

Google Labs spec

google-labs-code/design.md · Apache-2.0
  • YAML frontmatter with the tokens: at minimum colors, typography, spacing and rounded.
  • Markdown prose with the why behind each decision.
  • Eight sections in a fixed order: Overview, Colors, Typography, Layout, Elevation & Depth, Shapes, Components, Do's and Don'ts. They can be omitted, never reordered.
  • Path configurable in .agents/project.yaml → design_md_path (default ./DESIGN.md).
The exit gate

Lint before closing

npx @google/design.md lint <path>
  • Exits 0, with the WCAG AA contrast included in the check.
  • The frontmatter parses and all eight sections are there.
  • If something fails, the report says so. The AI does not invent fixes: it asks you how to go on.
If DESIGN.md already exists, the skill offers you skip (leaves it), overwrite (replaces it) or variant (writes DESIGN.<slug>.md next to it, for light/dark or an A/B test). A surgical rebrand updates tokens in place and keeps the order and the prose; it never regenerates the file from scratch.
Pauses that survive the session: paths C and D wait for work of yours outside. The AI leaves the checkpoint in .session/design-system/progress.md; when you return, it shows you the chosen path and the next step, and offers to resume, restart or abort.
After the lint: it goes back to /project-foundation (phase 3, SRS) if it came from there. /project-bootstrap reads DESIGN.md when it sets up the frontend, and without it, it stops. And the AI asks whether you also want to map screens: that is already the screen phase, always opt-in.
02·c · extract mode

Three modes, not two: the app already has a look

An app that adopts agentic-dev already looks some way. Picking a brand from the catalog would contradict what its users see every day. /design-system extract reads the live theme (the Tailwind config, the stylesheet the layout imports, components.json) and writes it as DESIGN.md, value by value, each one with the file it came from.

Mode When What it leaves
tokens New product: choose or write an identity. DESIGN.md through one of the five paths (§02).
extract Existing app: record the identity it already has. DESIGN.md mirroring the live theme, with its origin on every value.
screen One story, one mockup, right before building it. The spec and the Story → Screen row of the master design plan (§03).
What it does

Code → DESIGN.md, in one direction only

  • It resolves the live files by evidence: a dead copy (a globals.css nobody imports) is listed and ignored.
  • It copies each value in its own color format: the linter accepts oklch() and hsl(), so there is no conversion or rounding.
  • It computes derived radii (calc(...)) to a flat dimension, because the linter rejects them, and keeps the original expression in the comment.
What it never does

Touch the app or make things up

  • It does not touch the app code: it can only change DESIGN.md, an accepted variant and .session/design-system/.
  • It does not invent a token it cannot find: what is missing stays as a Discovery Gap.
  • It does not "fix" a low-contrast pair: it reports it, because the fix is a UI change with its own story.
After extract, never /project-bootstrap frontend. That phase generates the Tailwind config and the stylesheet from the tokens; on an app whose theme is the source, it would overwrite the live config with its own echo. The extracted file is consumed by reading it: /sprint-development uses it as the tokens contract in every UI story. Details: .agents/skills/design-system/references/extract-from-code.md.
03 · the loop

One mockup per story, right before building it

Not all screens are designed at once at the start. They are designed just-in-time: the screens of a feature are drawn right before its sprint. That way the blueprint arrives fresh and already aligned with the live UI.

Every new user story that renders interface should have its own mockup, whenever possible. Purely backend stories have no screen (they are marked with a dash in the map). When /sprint-development hits a UI story with no row in §8, it runs the design gate: it stops and lets you choose between three routes (below). It is Rule 14 (UI Fidelity Contract) of the repo.

The story enters the sprint

You pick up a UI story. /sprint-development looks for it in the Story → Screen map (§8 of the master design plan).

Is there a mockup? If not, you pick a route

With no row in §8, or with the row marked 🔒 mockup-gated, the flow stops and offers you three routes. The first is to go back to /design-system screen to produce the mockup for that batch of screens, with the handoff pattern of §04.

The mockup becomes a contract

You ratify each mockup, whether the AI commissioned it or you brought it. The AI reads the ratified ones, writes the screen spec (§4) and adds the row to the map (§8), and shows you the list so you can confirm it before saving.

It is built against the blueprint

Only then does /sprint-development implement the story, opening the spec in §4 and the tokens in §2. The fidelity reference is the live UI plus the DESIGN.md tokens: departing from them without ratifying it in §5 is a review defect.

Live-UI-first: the mockup is inspiration, not gospel. The current live UI plus the DESIGN.md tokens are the source of truth for fidelity: the components that already exist are reused and the mockup adapts to them, not the other way around. Following the live UI where the mockup differs is not a defect. If the mockup brings something genuinely good that the live UI does not have, it is not forced into the current story: it is filed as a future tech-story.
03·b · the design gate

It runs in Stage 1 of /sprint-development, on every story with UI, before planning code. That skill does not design: it checks and routes.

What it finds What happens
Row in §8 and its mockup It gets built: spec in §4, tokens in §2. A deliberate deviation is ratified in §5 before coding.
Row in §8 marked 🔒 mockup-gated The row already says what is missing. You pick one of the three routes.
No row in §8 The same three routes, and the row gets written too.
The project has no master design plan Fidelity from DESIGN.md only: token check, no reference screen. The Stage 1 plan says so.
Route What it is When
(a) Just-in-time mockup Goes to /design-system screen: brief, the highest method available (§04·b), bundle back. The default route, and the one offered first.
(b) Spec-only build, ratified in §5 It is built from the spec and recorded as a divergence in §5, plus an ADR if the deviation is architectural. No design tool is reachable, or the screen is trivial compared with patterns that already exist.
(c) DESIGN.md only Token fidelity for this story, with no reference screen. With your explicit approval.
You choose the route. The AI stops and shows you the three; it never picks silently.
When the order is reversed: if the screen encodes an irreversible decision or one that changes the scope, that decision is ratified in an ADR before drawing the mockup. A delete confirmation dialog is the delete policy: if it is drawn first, the policy was chosen by whoever composed the screen. It is checked before routing to (a).
03·c · after the build: the post-write pass

The design set and modern-web-guidance load before the first line of a screen. Once the screen is built, two more checks run on the result, in /sprint-development.

Before the write

modern-web-guidance picks the native feature (dialog, popover, view transitions, form validation) and its Baseline-aware fallback: search, then retrieve on the best match. It reads the project's Baseline target first.

After the write

The two checks below run on the built screen, in the Stage 2 implementer and again in the Stage 3 verifier.

Findings

Each one is fixed, or kept on purpose with its reason written down. The critique notes and the scan output go to .session/sprint-development/<KEY>/evidence/ so the PR can cite them.

Check What it looks at
/impeccable critique The live screen: hierarchy, states, responsive behaviour and copy.
modern-web-guidance legacy-scan The changed UI files: legacy patterns that the web platform now covers natively.
A PR that touches UI carries both. The review treats missing critique notes or a missing scan output as a finding. The skill is installed at project level by the installer and bun sync keeps it current.
04 · the universal pattern

How a mockup is handed to the AI

This handoff is identical in shape for every tool; what changes is where you draw the screens (step 2) and, for Open Design, who does steps 3 and 4 (you, or the AI alone via MCP, see §06).

You give the opt-in

You run /design-system screen, or the design gate brings you here. The phase always asks, it never runs on its own, with three options: yes I have/will produce mockups, no (the tokens are enough), or not yet.

you → "yes, I will produce mockups for these stories"

The AI hands you a BRIEF

The AI writes BRIEF.md seeded with the DESIGN.md tokens (real values inline) + the stories and visible behaviors. It is the portable prompt you will paste into the tool, or that the AI itself will use if the path is MCP-driven.

.context/designs/<project>/<batch>/BRIEF.md

You design in the external tool (or the AI commissions it via MCP)

You paste the brief into Claude Design / Open Design / whichever, iterate the screens by chatting, and export the result. On the manual path, you do this step yourself. In Open Design with MCP connected, the AI can trigger and poll the run on its own: see Mode A in §06.

The bundle lands in the "drop zone"

The exported files stay in the same folder as the brief. That is the delivery zone the AI watches (or fills itself, in Mode A).

.context/designs/<project>/<batch>/

The AI builds the contract

You tell it you are done (or it detects it when its run finishes). You ratify each mockup; the AI reads the ratified ones, lists the screens, writes §4 + §8, and shows you the map to review before saving it. Ready for the sprint.

you → "I left the mockups in the folder, build the master design plan"
Resume-safe: while you design (manual Mode B), the AI leaves a checkpoint and pauses. You can close the session: when you come back it resumes exactly where it was, waiting for your bundle. It does not invent screens just to "keep going".
04·b · who produces the screens, in order of preference

The AI uses the highest method the session has and notes which one in the batch's BRIEF.md.

Open Design via MCP (Mode A, preferred)

The AI commissions one run per screen, does QA and exports to the batch. The one that respects the tokens most and asks you for the least back and forth. Details in §06.

Claude Design or any prototyper (Mode B)

It hands you the brief to paste into the tool and pauses with session resume. You design and export to the batch.

The Claude Code design skill

Where the session has it: a canvas of .dc.html artboards that you can refine afterwards, by hand.

AI-written HTML with a design skill (Mode C)

The AI writes the screen HTML, but only with the design set loaded before the first line: frontend-design, ui-ux-pro-max, impeccable, emil-design-eng and modern-web-guidance (native web features and the project's Baseline target), plus redesign-existing-projects if it redesigns a screen that already exists and design-taste-frontend if it is a public marketing page. The skills are named in the BRIEF.md, the values stay inside the tokens contract, it is exported to the batch and you ratify it like any other mockup. If one is missing it says so once and goes on with the rest; with none loaded, this method does not exist.

If none of the four is available: STOP. The AI does not write the mockup by hand, without a design skill loaded (D7). Markup written to fill the gap looks like a design artifact but was never designed, and then everyone cites it as a spec. The legitimate way out is route (b) or (c) of the design gate: a spec-only build ratified in §5, or DESIGN.md only with your OK. Building from the tokens is fine; calling the result a mockup is not.
A logic prototype is inspiration only. When a screen hides a rule that cannot be judged by looking at an image (a state machine, a pricing or permissions model), a throwaway prototype answers that question before the mockup is commissioned. Its result goes to the ADR or the story; it never counts as a mockup or a spec.
In any method: never a color, a font, a spacing step or a radius outside the tokens contract of DESIGN.md.

05·b · step by step, for screen mockups

You open a project and paste the BRIEF

You create a project (it inherits your org's design system). You plug in context: GitHub repo or local directory, existing assets. You paste the whole BRIEF.md as the first chat message.

You iterate in four ways

Chat for big changes · inline comments (click an element on the canvas) · direct editing (drag / resize / align) · sliders that Claude generates for spacing, color and layout.

You cover the states and several screens

One project holds the complete flow (several screens connected with navigation). It generates the edge states (empty, loading, error) before the handoff.

You export: two routes

Export button (top right): "Send to local coding agent" pushes the bundle straight to your local Claude Code running in the repo, or "Save as folder" downloads it as a ZIP/folder for you to move by hand.

You confirm the destination

With "Save as folder": unzip into .context/designs/<project>/<batch>/. With "Send to local coding agent": you tell the agent that same destination path; the scope of this drop zone is screen mockups, not the DESIGN.md bundle (that one uses design/handoff/).

06·b · two modes, same destination
Mode A · preferred

MCP-driven

mcp__open-design__*

With the Open Design daemon connected as an MCP server, the AI itself calls create_project + start_run with the BRIEF.md content, polls get_run every 30–60s in a foreground loop, and on seeing status: succeeded it locates the result with get_artifact / list_files and copies it (cp) from the Open Design data root to the repo's drop zone, without you touching the window. You ratify the exported screens before they enter the map.

Mode B · fallback

Manual + PAUSE

manual handoff

If the MCP is not connected: the AI leaves the BRIEF.md and pauses. You open the desktop app, paste the brief into Studio → New Project (skill/template + DESIGN.md + brief), iterate, and copy the final file from the project's data folder (or run od export) to the drop zone, and tell it when it is there.

06·c · install and connect the engine
# Recommended route: no Docker, signed binary Download the installer from the nexu-io/open-design releases (macOS/Win/Linux) and open the app. No fixed port or host.docker.internal needed. # Docker still exists, but it is no longer the recommended route, # only if you already live in containers or need remote access.

You configure the engine

In Settings you choose how it generates: pointing to your already-authenticated CLI (Claude Code / Codex / OpenCode, without an extra key) or loading an API key for any endpoint compatible with the OpenAI API (BYOK).

You connect the MCP to your agent

You register the Open Design daemon as an MCP server in your coding agent (Claude Code, etc.). Once connected, the mcp__open-design__* tools become available and Mode A becomes the default path.

You pick the 3 inputs and fire

An OD skill (for application screens use frontend-design) + design system (your real DESIGN.md) + a natural-language brief (the BRIEF.md). Send, by hand in Studio, or via start_run if the AI is the one firing.

The artifact streams live

Open Design's internal agent emits a complete, self-contained HTML; the right panel streams it as a navigable prototype: buttons, tabs and modals work. Each follow-up creates a new version, the previous ones stay in the history.

Closing the handoff

Mode A: the AI locates the file with get_artifact and copies it (cp) to the drop zone; the MCP does not write outside its own data root. Mode B: you copy the file (or run od export) to .context/designs/<project>/<batch>/ and tell it.

06·d · gotchas verified in production

If you are going to operate Open Design via MCP (Mode A), these four signals are confusing if you do not know them beforehand.

The agent's message lies before the file doesThe self-report (agentMessage) can report the wrong language or result even though the generated HTML is correct. The file on disk is the ground truth: grep it, do not react to the message.
"produced no files" can be a false hintWith artifactCount: 1 and the file already written, the get_run hint sometimes says otherwise. The filesystem rules.
updatedAt freezes during extended thinkingMinutes without movement in the run timestamp are not a hang: it is the model thinking. Check real liveness against the last event in events.jsonl.
Poll foreground only, never a bare sleep in the backgroundA subagent that waits for a run with a long sleep in the background stalls itself. Proven pattern: a foreground loop with a timeout and a short sleep inside, never a loose sleep.
Key detail: Open Design consumes DESIGN.md, it does not replace it, but it is not automatic. First you have to install the repo's design system as a user package (user:<slug>) and publish it (it starts as draft, projects cannot use it until it is published). Without that, screens may come out with invented values instead of your real palette and typography.
07 · other routes

Any prototyper works

The repo treats anything that lands in the drop zone as a screen source. Claude Design and Open Design are the recommended ones, but the universal pattern (§04) also accepts:

Figma

static export

You design in Figma as always and export the screens (images / HTML / specs) to the drop zone. The AI reads them as a visual reference. Ideal if your team already lives in Figma. The downside: they are not live HTML prototypes, so the AI infers layout from the image + your brief.

Manual HTML

no visual tool

You write it yourself, by hand, or by pasting the output of any AI chat you use as a notepad, in another session, with no connection to the one running /design-system. Fast and without leaving the terminal. The downside: no visual canvas to iterate comfortably, refinement is by chat in that other session and by checking the rendered HTML.

Hard rule, delegate and do not generate: the session that runs /design-system never writes the mockup itself without a design skill loaded (no Write / write_file on design HTML: anti-patterns S1 of screen-design-mapping.md, D7 of SKILL.md, B4 of screen-design-brief.md). If the HTML comes from an AI, there are two paths: it comes from another session, treated exactly like a Figma export (a an external file that you drop into the drop zone), or this same session writes it through a frontend-ui design skill (Mode C), named in the BRIEF.md. What never exists is markup that the orchestrating session writes "while it waits", with no design skill at all.
07·b · the manual route, step by step

The fastest option when you do not want to start any app. It works well for simple screens and for iterating quickly before polishing in a visual tool, as long as the file is produced and moved by you, not by the /design-system session.

You produce the first screen, separately

In another window or session (or by hand), a prompt that anchors three things: which screen, which story, and that it uses the DESIGN.md tokens. You ask for a self-contained file (openable in the browser right away).

you, in that other session → "Generate a self-contained HTML mockup of the login screen for story PROJ-14. Use the tokens from DESIGN.md. A single file."

You save it in the drop zone yourself

You copy the resulting .html to .context/designs/<project>/<batch>/login.html: your own action, just like unzipping a Claude Design zip. You open it and look at it in the browser: that render is your canvas.

You iterate in that other session

You describe the change in words there; that session rewrites the file; you copy it back to the drop zone and refresh the browser. Repeat until it is right.

you, in that other session → "the primary button bigger, add the error state and a forgot-password link"

You cover the edge states

Ask explicitly for empty, loading and error: they are not added on their own. They are part of the screen contract.

You return to the /design-system session and close the handoff

The file is already in the drop zone (you put it there in step 2). You ask /design-system to build the plan. Same ending as Claude Design or Open Design.

you → "the mockup is ready, build the master design plan for PROJ-14"
The principle does not change: wherever the mockup comes from, it ends up in .context/designs/<project>/<batch>/ placed there by you, and the AI turns it into a screen spec + a row in the Story→Screen map. The tool is interchangeable; who produces it may vary; the contract (and who moves it to the drop zone) is always the same.

09 · what you tell the AI

The exact commands

The skill name plus the mode, tokens or screen. With no mode, the AI takes it from the request (a story that needs a screen is screen; brand, tokens or DESIGN.md is tokens) and, if it is still unclear, it asks.

Claude Code

/design-system tokens /design-system screen PROJ-14

Slash command for the skill, the mode and what follows (the story) arrive intact.

OpenCode

load the design-system skill, tokens mode

No slash command: you name the skill and the mode in the sentence.

Codex

load the design-system skill, screen mode, story PROJ-14

Same pattern as OpenCode, CLI or Desktop.

Pi

/skill:design-system tokens

Slash command with the /skill: prefix. In a new folder, /trust once.

You start the screen phase

you → /design-system screen PROJ-14

And when it asks about the screen mapping: "yes, I will produce the mockups".

You receive the brief, design outside (or let the AI operate Open Design)

The AI leaves BRIEF.md in the batch folder. Mode B: you paste it into Claude Design or Open Design, iterate, export the bundle to that same folder. Mode A (Open Design + MCP): you do not need to do anything else, the AI fires and polls on its own.

You close the handoff

you → "I already left the mockups in .context/designs/<project>/<batch>/, build the master design plan"

The AI reads, writes §4 + §8 and shows you the Story→Screen map so you can confirm it.

You build the story

you → "implement story PROJ-XX"  ·  /sprint-development

If that story had no mockup, the design gate stops and offers you the three routes; (a) takes you back to step 1 with a brief only for that batch of screens.

In one sentence: /design-system tokens once → DESIGN.md. Per story: /design-system screen (opt-in) → BRIEF.md → you design outside or the AI commissions Open Design via MCP → you ratify → the AI builds the plan → /sprint-development builds against the live UI and the tokens.
10 · before you start

Preconditions, in a list

Before you start producing mockups, have this ready:

DESIGN.md exists. Mockups are seeded with its frozen tokens. Without it there is no visual contract: run /design-system tokens first.
Backlog with user stories. They are needed to seed the brief and build the Story→Screen map.
Tool chosen and ready. Claude Design (Pro+ plan) · Open Design (desktop app installed, MCP connected if you want Mode A) · Figma · the Claude Code design skill · or a frontend-ui design skill loaded (Mode C). With none, the design gate offers you (b) or (c), never a mockup written by the AI without a design skill.
Opt-in given. The screens phase is always requested explicitly; it never runs on its own.
Clear drop zone. You know in which .context/designs/<project>/<batch>/ the bundle will land, and that it is not the same folder as design/handoff/.
11 · start now

Starting from zero

Literal runbook. Two paths depending on how much you want to set up. Both start by opening Claude Code, OpenCode, Codex or Pi at the repo root; the skills live in .agents/skills/ and every terminal reads them the same way (Claude Code through the generated alias .claude/skills), so /design-system and /sprint-development are available in any of the three.

Path A · full flow with the skill

With a visual tool

When you are going to design in Claude Design or Open Design and want the BRIEF seeded with your tokens.

# repo root claude # or: opencode, codex, pi # inside the session (in Pi: /skill:design-system screen; in OpenCode or Codex: "load the design-system skill, screen mode"): /design-system screen PROJ-XX # answer the screens opt-in: "yes, I will produce the mockups"

The AI leaves the BRIEF.md, you paste it into your tool, export the bundle to the drop zone, and close with "build the master design plan".

Path B · manual HTML, no external app

You produce the HTML, separately

The fastest to get going. You do not start anything, but you generate and move the file yourself, in a different session from the one running /design-system.

# in ANOTHER window/session, not the repo one with /design-system claude # or: opencode, or any AI chat # seed prompt (adjust screen + story): "Generate a self-contained HTML mockup of the <X> screen for story PROJ-XX. Use the tokens from DESIGN.md. Include the empty, loading and error states. A single file." # you copy the result to the drop zone: cp screen.html .context/designs/<project>/<batch>/<X>.html

You open the .html in the browser, iterate by chat in that other session, and only then go back to the /design-system session and close with "build the master design plan for PROJ-XX".

Rule for the prompt: always anchor screen + story (PROJ-XX) + "use the DESIGN.md tokens". With those anchors the mockup comes out aligned, and you are the one who brings it to the drop zone for the rest of the flow, never the orchestrating session.
When each path: Path B for simple screens and for quick tests before committing. Path A when the screen is rich and you want the visual refinement (canvas, sliders, comments) of Claude Design, or the end-to-end automation of Open Design in Mode A. You can start in B and move to A without losing anything: the same BRIEF.md works for both.

The core idea. The system is fixed once, in DESIGN.md. The blueprint of each screen comes later, story by story: a design tool produces it (commissioned by the AI or used by you), you ratify it, and the AI maps it to the story and builds against the live UI and the tokens, with the mockup as inspiration. The AI never writes the blueprint by hand without a design skill.
